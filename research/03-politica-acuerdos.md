## vstg-0081 — Treaty of Sèvres, 1920

- Código visible: vstg.1920.b
- Concepción: 1920 · Cierre: 1923 · Distancia a 2020: 100
- [desc.] The peace treaty signed on 10 August 1920 between the Allies and the Ottoman government. It partitioned most of the empire, set up an independent Armenia and a referendum for Kurdistan, and gave Smyrna to Greek administration.
- [f.f. expl.] An Anatolia split between mandates, zones and small states, with a Kurdistan that might have existed. The Turkish national movement rejected the treaty and won the war; the borders were redrawn at Lausanne in 1923.
- [Efecto en el presente — attributed] The 'Sèvres syndrome', the popular belief in Turkey that internal and external enemies conspire to carve up the republic, takes its name from the treaty. Devlet Bahçeli invoked it in 2015, Erdoğan said in 2019 'we overturned the Treaty of Sèvres', and Le Monde (31 July 2020) linked the opening of Hagia Sophia for worship to a 'new Treaty of Sèvres', which İbrahim Karagül answered in Yeni Şafak.
- Fuente del efecto: Wikipedia, "Sèvres syndrome" (citing Le Monde, 31 July 2020). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A8vres_syndrome
- Fuente: Wikipedia, "Treaty of Sèvres". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_S%C3%A8vres
- Nota: Efecto presente atribuido vía Wikipedia (Bahçeli 2015, Erdoğan 2019, Le Monde 2020); los originales no se han consultado.

## vstg-0082 — Anglo-French Union, 1940

- Código visible: vstg.1940.a
- Concepción: 1940 · Cierre: 1940 · Distancia a 2020: 80
- [desc.] The British offer of 16 June 1940 to France to merge the two countries into one "indissoluble" union, with a joint war cabinet, joint organs for defence, foreign and economic policy, and reciprocal citizenship.
- [f.f. expl.] Two nations that would have become one state in the middle of a collapse. The union stayed as a text, and the offer was never put into effect.
- [Efecto en el presente — attributed] The idea resurfaced in 1956, when Guy Mollet proposed a union with Elizabeth II as head of state and Anthony Eden rejected it. It became public through a BBC article by Mike Thomson on 15 January 2007, which coined the name 'Frangleterre'; on 16 January 2007 Charles Pasqua said on French television that a formal request would have led to Mollet being tried for high treason.
- Fuente del efecto: Wikipedia, "Franco-British Union" (citing BBC, 15 January 2007). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-British_Union
- Fuente: Anglo-French Declaration, 16 June 1940. Text at ibiblio.org (Policy Archive). https://www.ibiblio.org/pha/policy/1940/400616a.html
- Nota: Efecto presente débil: el eco más reciente es de 2007. Verificado en Wikipedia.

## vstg-0083 — Gran Colombia, 1819-1831

- Código visible: vstg.1831
- Concepción: 1819 · Cierre: 1831 · Distancia a 2020: 201
- [desc.] The republic proclaimed in 1819 at the Congress of Angostura and shaped by the Constitution of Cúcuta (1821), covering present-day Colombia, mainland Ecuador, Panama and Venezuela.

## vstg-0084 — Baruch Plan, 1946

- Código visible: vstg.1946.a
- Concepción: 1946 · Cierre: 1947 · Distancia a 2020: 74
- [desc.] The US proposal, presented by Bernard Baruch to the UN Atomic Energy Commission on 14 June 1946, to place nuclear material under an international authority, with sanctions that the Security Council could not veto.
- [f.f. expl.] A deal that would have given up the American monopoly on the bomb, if the Soviets accepted inspection in return. The Soviet Union refused, and the Baruch offer stayed on paper while the arms race started.
- [Efecto en el presente — attributed] Wikipedia says the plan is often cited in works promoting the internationalisation of nuclear power or revisiting arms control, and Nick Bostrom cited it in Superintelligence (2014) as an example of how a power with a decisive strategic advantage might establish a benign 'singleton'.
- Fuente del efecto: Wikipedia, "Baruch Plan" (citing Bostrom, Superintelligence, 2014). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baruch_Plan
- Fuente: Baruch, B. (1946), UN Atomic Energy Commission. Wikipedia, "Baruch Plan". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baruch_Plan
- Nota: Efecto presente atribuido (Bostrom 2014) vía Wikipedia; la ficha no vincula el plan con el OIEA ni con el TNP.

## vstg-0085 — Havana Charter and the International Trade Organization

- Código visible: vstg.1948.b
- Concepción: 1946 · Cierre: 1950 · Distancia a 2020: 74
- [desc.] The charter signed in Havana on 24 March 1948 to create the International Trade Organization, with rules on trade and on anti-competitive practices. The US government stopped seeking Senate approval in 1950.
- [f.f. expl.] A world trade body with its own rules, which was never created. The GATT took its place as a looser club, and the trade order of the postwar decades was built on that compromise.
- [Efecto en el presente — documented] Elements of the Havana Charter became part of the GATT, which gradually became the main forum of trade cooperation. After the Uruguay Round of 1994 the World Trade Organization replaced the GATT in 1995 and adopted its principles and agreements.

## vstg-0086 — European Defence Community, 1952–1954

- Código visible: vstg.1952.a
- Concepción: 1950 · Cierre: 1954 · Distancia a 2020: 70
- [desc.] The treaty signed on 27 May 1952 to create a single European army, under a common budget, within NATO. The French National Assembly ended its ratification in August 1954.
- [f.f. expl.] An army of Europe that would never be built. The French parliament postponed ratification indefinitely, and the idea of a common defence was shelved for decades.
- [Efecto en el presente — attributed] Wikipedia says the EU and NATO carry out some functions the EDC envisaged, though neither approaches its supranational military control. Federico Fabbrini (2024) and a 2025 study led by Sylvie Goulard argued that ratification remains legally feasible, and on 3 April 2025 Mauro Del Barba tabled a bill in the Italian Parliament to ratify the treaty.

## vstg-0087 — Stalin Note, 1952

- Código visible: vstg.1952.b
- Concepción: 1952 · Cierre: 1952 · Distancia a 2020: 68
- [desc.] The Soviet note of 10 March 1952 proposing a reunified, neutral Germany under a peace treaty, with the withdrawal of occupation forces within a year. The Western powers answered on 25 March 1952 by asking for free elections first.
- [f.f. expl.] A Germany that might have been neutral and whole, which the West refused. Adenauer chose integration into the Western alliance, and the division of Germany became permanent for another four decades.
- [Efecto en el presente — attributed] Historians still dispute whether a chance for a neutral, reunified Germany was missed. Rolf Steininger argued in 1985 that Stalin's offer was serious and that Adenauer blocked its testing; Hermann Graml argued the note was an alibi for integrating East Germany into the Eastern bloc. The debate recurred when Western archives opened in the 1980s and Eastern ones in the 1990s, with Zarusky's 2002 volume and Ruggenthaler's 2011 review.
- Fuente del efecto: Wikipedia, "Stalin Note" (citing Steininger, 1985; Graml; Zarusky, 2002; Ruggenthaler, 2011). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalin_Note
- Fuente: Wikipedia, "Stalin Note". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalin_Note
- Nota: Efecto presente atribuido (debate historiográfico) vía Wikipedia; la ficha dice que el debate no intervino en la reunificación de 1990.

## vstg-0088 — European Political Community, 1952–1954

- Código visible: vstg.1954
- Concepción: 1952 · Cierre: 1954 · Distancia a 2020: 68
- [desc.] The political community proposed in 1952 to bring the European Coal and Steel Community and the European Defence Community under one bicameral parliament and a supranational executive. It was abandoned in 1954, after the French National Assembly stalled the EDC.
- [f.f. expl.] A federal Europe that would have had a parliament before it had a constitution. It failed together with the defence treaty it was meant to crown.

## vstg-0090 — Mali Federation, 1959–1960

- Código visible: vstg.1959.b
- Concepción: 1959 · Cierre: 1960 · Distancia a 2020: 61
- [desc.] A federation of French Sudan and Senegal, founded on 4 April 1959 within the French Community. Senegal left on 20 August 1960 and the federation dissolved after about four months.
- [f.f. expl.] An independent West African state that would have joined two former colonies in one country. It lasted barely a year and a half: two rival leaders and two armies ended it.

## vstg-0093 — Kellogg-Briand Pact, 1928

- Código visible: vstg.1928.c
- Concepción: 1927 · Cierre: n/d · Distancia a 2020: 93
- [desc.] Signed in Paris on 27 August 1928 and in effect from 24 July 1929, by which signatories renounced war as an instrument of national policy.
- [f.f. expl.] A world in which war was outlawed by treaty. It had no enforcement mechanism; many historians consider it largely ineffective, and Eric Sevareid called it a 'worthless piece of paper'.
- [Efecto en el presente — documented] The pact remains in effect: Barbados (1971), Fiji (1973) and successor states of Czechoslovakia and Yugoslavia in the 1990s joined later. The Nuremberg and Tokyo tribunals used it as the basis of 'crimes against peace', and its central provisions were incorporated into Article 2(4) of the UN Charter. Hathaway and Shapiro argue it inaugurated 'a new era of human history'.
- Fuente del efecto: Wikipedia, "Kellogg-Briand Pact". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kellogg%E2%80%93Briand_Pact
- Fuente: Wikipedia, "Kellogg-Briand Pact". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kellogg%E2%80%93Briand_Pact
- Nota: Sustituto verificado en Wikipedia. Año de concepción: 1927 (negociación Kellogg-Briand), no confirmado en la ficha.

## vstg-0094 — Sunningdale Agreement, 1973–1974

- Código visible: vstg.1974.b
- Concepción: 1973 · Cierre: 1974 · Distancia a 2020: 47
- [desc.] The December 1973 agreement at Sunningdale Park that created a power-sharing executive for Northern Ireland with a cross-border council. The Ulster Workers' Council strike of May 1974 brought the executive down on 28 May.
- [f.f. expl.] A Northern Ireland government shared between unionists and nationalists. A general strike of a few weeks ended it; the government had no will to use force against it, and the power-sharing idea waited two decades.

## vstg-0095 — New International Economic Order, 1974

- Código visible: vstg.1974.c
- Concepción: 1974 · Cierre: n/d · Distancia a 2020: 46
- [desc.] The UN General Assembly declaration of 1 May 1974 calling for a New International Economic Order: fair prices for raw materials, technology transfer and sovereignty over natural resources for developing countries.
- [f.f. expl.] A world economy rebalanced for the countries that had been exporting raw materials for two centuries. Adopted by the UN; the debt crisis of the 1980s and the market turn made it impossible to realise.
- [Efecto en el presente — documented] The Common Fund for Commodities came into force in 1989, one of the parts of the NIEO that were realised. A 2018 UN resolution, 'Towards a New International Economic Order', reaffirmed the need to keep working toward one, and Progressive International published updated proposals in September 2024.

## vstg-0096 — Keynes's Bancor, 1940-1944

- Código visible: vstg.1944.b
- Concepción: 1940 · Cierre: 1944 · Distancia a 2020: 80
- [desc.] Keynes's proposed unit of account for an International Clearing Union, conceived between 1940 and 1942 and adopted as the UK's official proposal at the Bretton Woods Conference.
- [f.f. expl.] A postwar monetary order in which surpluses and deficits both carried penalties, so that countries would be pushed toward balanced trade. It was not accepted at Bretton Woods.
- [Efecto en el presente — attributed] Zhou Xiaochuan, governor of the People's Bank of China, called Keynes's approach 'farsighted' in a 2009 speech and proposed IMF special drawing rights as a global reserve currency; US Treasury Secretary Timothy Geithner expressed interest, was heavily criticised, and the administration backtracked. A UN expert commission report (2009) and an IMF study (April 2010) made similar analyses.
- Fuente del efecto: Wikipedia, "Bancor" (citing Zhou Xiaochuan, 2009). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bancor
- Fuente: Wikipedia, "Bancor". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bancor
- Nota: Sustituto: efecto atribuido (Zhou Xiaochuan 2009) vía Wikipedia; la ficha no menciona los DEG de 1969.

## vstg-0098 — Meech Lake Accord, 1987–1990

- Código visible: vstg.1990.b
- Concepción: 1987 · Cierre: 1990 · Distancia a 2020: 33
- [desc.] A package of constitutional amendments agreed by Canada's first ministers at Meech Lake in 1987, recognising Quebec as a distinct society. Manitoba and Newfoundland did not ratify it before the June 1990 deadline.
- [f.f. expl.] A constitution that would have brought Quebec into the national compact. It lapsed in a single night in June 1990, when a legislator withheld consent and a premier judged the vote lost; the country's constitutional debate moved to a new round.

## vstg-0104 — Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty, 1996

- Código visible: vstg.1996
- Concepción: 1996 · Cierre: n/d · Distancia a 2020: 24
- [desc.] The treaty signed on 24 September 1996 banning all nuclear explosions. It has not entered into force: nine further Annex 2 states must ratify it, including the United States, China and Russia.
- [f.f. expl.] A world in which no nuclear explosion would be tested, verified by a global network. Opened for signature on 24 September 1996, it has not entered into force: nine listed states have still to ratify it.
- [Efecto en el presente — documented] The treaty has still not entered into force: as of 2024, nine more states must ratify it (China, North Korea, Egypt, India, Iran, Israel, Pakistan, Russia and the United States, as the Arms Control Association lists them). The Preparatory Commission of the CTBTO and its International Monitoring System keep operating, and Article XIV conferences continue to meet.
