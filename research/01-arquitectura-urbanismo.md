## vstg-0001 — Monument to the Third International, 1919–1920

**Código visible:** vstg.1920  ·  **Concepción:** 1919  ·  **Cierre:** —  ·  **Distancia a 2020:** 101 años  ·  **Nivel:** documented

**Estado:** Efecto presente verificado en Wikipedia con tres fuentes citadas (UEA 2017, The Guardian 2017, RBC 2021); pendiente abrir las dos últimas en el original.

**[desc.]** Model of a 400-metre double helix of iron and glass designed by Vladimir Tatlin in 1919–1920 as the headquarters of the Communist International in Petrograd. Only models were built.

**[f.f. expl.]** A tower meant to be both headquarters and monument of a new international order. It was never built; only models were made.

**[efecto en el presente — documented]** Models are held at the Moderna Museet, the Tretyakov Gallery and the Centre Pompidou. A 1:42 model was built at the Royal Academy in 2011 and at the Sainsbury Centre in 2017, and has stood in its sculpture park since 2018. Ai Weiwei's Fountain of Light (2007) at the Louvre Abu Dhabi is modelled on the tower, and the SKA Arena in Saint Petersburg (2023) was designed with inspiration from it.

## vstg-0008 — Fordlândia, 1928

**Código visible:** vstg.1928.A  ·  **Concepción:** 1928  ·  **Cierre:** 1945  ·  **Distancia a 2020:** 92 años  ·  **Nivel:** documented

**Estado:** Verificada con Wikipedia; pendiente abrir NPR 2009 y The Guardian 2016. La fuente no da una referencia para los detalles de los edificios.

**[desc.]** Henry Ford's company established Fordlândia in the Amazon rainforest in 1928 to secure cultivated rubber for its cars.

**[f.f. expl.]** A model town to end Ford's dependence on European rubber. Henry Ford II sold it back to the Brazilian government in 1945 at a loss of over $20 million, and, as NPR reported in 2009, 'Not one drop of latex from Fordlândia ever made it into a Ford car.'

Most original buildings remain: the 50-metre water tower still operates, and the workshop, sawmill and kiln still stand. The hospital was dismantled by looters.

## vstg-0012 — Pruitt–Igoe, St. Louis

**Código visible:** vstg.1951  ·  **Concepción:** 1951  ·  **Cierre:** 1972  ·  **Distancia a 2020:** 69 años  ·  **Nivel:** documented

**Estado:** Verificada con Wikipedia. Katharine Bristol (1991) y Eugene Meehan (1979) discuten las causas; la ficha no atribuye una causa única.

**[desc.]** The Pruitt–Igoe public housing complex in St. Louis was built between 1951 and 1955. The first tenants moved into Pruitt in November 1954 and into Igoe in July 1955.

**[f.f. expl.]** Modern public housing as the answer to the slum. The first building was demolished by explosives on 16 March 1972 and the site was fully cleared by 1976. Charles Jencks called the demolition 'the day Modern architecture died' (The Language of Post-Modern Architecture, 1984, p. 9).

An elementary school (1995), a planned medical facility (2020) and an urgent care centre (2022) have been built or planned there. The case became a symbol of modernism's failures and a case study for Oscar Newman's defensible space theory.

**Código visible:** vstg.1973  ·  **Concepción:** pendiente  ·  **Cierre:** —  ·  **Distancia a 2020:** pendiente  ·  **Nivel:** documented
