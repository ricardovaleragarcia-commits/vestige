## vstg-0120 — Charles Babbage, Difference Engine, 1822–1833

- Código visible: vstg.1822
- Concepción: 1822 · Cierre: n/d · Distancia a 2020: 198
- [desc.] Charles Babbage began the Difference Engine in 1822, a machine to compute mathematical tables mechanically. It was never completed: government funding ended and the project's engineer fell out with Babbage.
- [f.f. expl.] A calculating machine that would have printed tables without human error. Unfinished and unfunded, it left the designs of a computer that Babbage did not live to build.
- [Efecto en el presente — documented] The Science Museum in London built a working Difference Engine from Babbage's plans between 1989 and 1991, returning results to 31 digits, and completed his printer in 2000. A second engine went on show at the Computer History Museum in California on 10 May 2008, and in 2011 researchers proposed 'Plan 28' to build the Analytical Engine.

- [desc.] Henry Moseley, the English physicist who showed that X-ray wavelengths follow a systematic relationship with atomic number. He was killed by a sniper at Gallipoli on 10 August 1915, aged 27.

- Concepción: 1856 · Cierre: n/d · Distancia a 2020: 164
- [desc.] Gregor Mendel presented his plant hybridisation experiments in Brno in February and March 1865 and published them in 1866. The paper was largely ignored and cited about three times in thirty-five years.
- [f.f. expl.] Laws of heredity filed away for thirty-five years. They were rediscovered in 1900 by three botanists who confirmed them independently.
