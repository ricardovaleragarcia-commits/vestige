## vstg-0156 — Solresol, the musical language

- Código visible: vstg.1817
- Concepción: 1817 · Cierre: n/d · Distancia a 2020: 203
- [desc.] Jean-François Sudre began presenting Solresol in 1817. It is built on the seven solfège syllables do, re, mi, fa, sol, la, si. His book Langue musicale universelle appeared posthumously in 1866.
- [f.f. expl.] A universal language that could be sung, whistled or played on instruments. Today small communities of enthusiasts keep it alive.
- [Efecto en el presente — documented] Stephen L. Rice published an English translation of Gajewski's Grammaire du Solrésol in 1997, Jason Hutchens released an English–Solresol dictionary in 1998, and Phil Shary released open-source translation software with live MIDI support in 2015. A team including Daniel Parson produced a modernised dictionary in 2018; a request for an ISO 639-3 code was rejected on 23 January 2018.

## vstg-0157 — Volapük, the first planned world language

- Código visible: vstg.1880
- Concepción: 1879 · Cierre: n/d · Distancia a 2020: 141
- [desc.] Johann Martin Schleyer published a sketch of Volapük in May 1879 and a full book in 1880. By 1889 there were an estimated 283 clubs, and the language claimed nearly a million adherents.
- [f.f. expl.] A global language that collapsed within a decade. Internal conflicts and schisms, together with the rise of Esperanto, drew speakers away.
- [Efecto en el presente — documented] The Volapük edition of Wikipedia reached over 112,000 articles in December 2007, mostly small geographic stubs created by an enthusiast's program (119,091 in March 2013). Unicode 7.0 (June 2014) encoded Volapük-specific letters, and Hermann Philipps has led the movement as Cifal since 2014.

## vstg-0158 — The Deseret alphabet

- Código visible: vstg.1854
- Concepción: 1854 · Cierre: 1870 · Distancia a 2020: 166
- [desc.] A 38-letter phonetic alphabet developed from 1847 by the University of Deseret's board of regents under Brigham Young, with George D. Watt designing most glyphs. The board approved it on 19 January 1854.
- [f.f. expl.] An alphabet meant to replace English spelling in Utah. Four books were printed in 1868–69, including the full Book of Mormon, but only about 500 copies sold and advertisements were quietly removed from the Deseret News in winter 1870.
- [Efecto en el presente — documented] Unicode added the script in March 2001 (version 3.1). Digital fonts and transcriptions have since revived it as a cultural heirloom, and a replica headstone in Cedar City is one modern-era example.

## vstg-0159 — The Mundaneum

- Código visible: vstg.1910
- Concepción: 1910 · Cierre: 1940 · Distancia a 2020: 110
- [desc.] An institution created in 1910 by the Belgian lawyers Paul Otlet and Henri La Fontaine to gather the world's knowledge and classify it under the Universal Decimal Classification; it first occupied a wing of the Cinquantenaire Palace in Brussels.
- [f.f. expl.] In 1934 Otlet proposed a network of 'electric telescopes' for searching linked documents. In 1940, after Nazi Germany invaded Belgium, the Mundaneum was replaced with an exhibit of Third Reich art and some material was lost; in 1972 it was forced to leave its Leopold Park building.
- [Efecto en el presente — documented] A museum opened in Mons in 1998, where the Mundaneum is now based. In 2012 the New York Times reported a planned Google venture with the museum, and on 23 August 2015 a Google Doodle honoured it.

- Concepción: 1917 · Cierre: 1932 · Distancia a 2020: 103
- [desc.] Proletkult was founded in October 1917; its first conference met in Petrograd from 16 to 19 October. In 1920 Lenin pushed through a decision to absorb it into the People's Commissariat of Education (Narkompros), with a Central Committee decree published on 1 December 1920.

## vstg-0164 — The League of Nations

- Código visible: vstg.1920.d
- Concepción: 1920 · Cierre: 1946 · Distancia a 2020: 100
- [desc.] The League of Nations' Covenant took effect with the Treaty of Versailles on 10 January 1920, and the Council first met on 16 January 1920.
- [f.f. expl.] An organisation meant to prevent war that did not enforce its own decisions. The United States never joined; Japan and Germany left in 1933, Italy in 1937 and Spain in 1939. The League lacked its own armed force.

## vstg-0166 — Yañalif, the Latin alphabet for Soviet Turkic languages

- Código visible: vstg.1927
- Concepción: 1927 · Cierre: 1940 · Distancia a 2020: 93
- [desc.] Yañalif, a Latin-based alphabet devised for Turkic languages, was adopted in Tatarstan in 1927; its final version came in 1928. It was used for Tatar and Bashkir and other Soviet languages.
- [f.f. expl.] Soviet Latinisation ended with a ban. The government prohibited Yañalif in 1939, and it remained in use until January 1940, when the switch to Cyrillic was complete. Learning two alphabets was considered problematic.
- [Efecto en el presente — documented] The Internet alphabet Inalif (2003) was partly inspired by Yañalif. Unicode recommends reusing Cyrillic Ь ь and Ѣ ѣ for Yañalif letters, and the IETF has registered the subtag 'baku1926' for text in this notation. After 1991 several former Soviet states in Central Asia returned to Latin scripts with slight modifications to Yañalif.

## vstg-0167 — The Shavian alphabet

- Código visible: vstg.1950
- Concepción: 1950 · Cierre: n/d · Distancia a 2020: 70
- [desc.] A phonetic alphabet called for in George Bernard Shaw's will of 1950. After his death in November 1950 and a legal dispute, the Public Trustee announced a design competition; four designers won and Ronald Kingsley Read combined their designs.
- [f.f. expl.] A new alphabet intended to replace English spelling. The only book the trust could afford was the bi-alphabetic Penguin edition of Androcles and the Lion in 1962; Read published eight issues of the journal Shaw-Script in 1963–1965.
- [Efecto en el presente — documented] Shavian was added to Unicode in version 4.0 in 2003. Tim Browne published Poe Meets Shaw in 2012, Thomas Thurman's Shavian transcription of Alice's Adventures in Wonderland appeared in 2013 with fonts by Michael Everson, and a Shavian-only edition of Pride and Prejudice was published by Shavian.info in 2019.

## vstg-0178 — Lincos, a language for cosmic intercourse

## vstg-0180 — Kazakhstan's Latin alphabet plan, postponed to 2031

- [desc.] Kazakhstan announced a switch from Cyrillic to the Latin alphabet in April 2017 and ordered it by presidential decree in October 2017, with the switch due by 2025.
