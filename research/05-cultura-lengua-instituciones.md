## vstg-0156 — Solresol, the musical language

**Año:** 1817  ·  **Nivel:** documented  ·  **Estado:** Verificada con Wikipedia: presentación en 1817; libro póstumo en 1866; siete sílabas del solfeo.

**[desc.]** Jean-François Sudre began presenting Solresol in 1817. It is built on the seven solfège syllables do, re, mi, fa, sol, la, si. His book Langue musicale universelle appeared posthumously in 1866.

**[f.f. expl.]** A universal language that could be sung, whistled or played on instruments. It had a brief spell of popularity in the late nineteenth century and today survives only in small groups of enthusiasts.

## vstg-0157 — Volapük, the first planned world language

**Año:** 1880  ·  **Nivel:** documented  ·  **Estado:** Verificada con Wikipedia: esbozo de mayo de 1879; libro de 1880; 283 clubes en 1889; declive por cismas y por el esperanto.

**[desc.]** Johann Martin Schleyer published a sketch of Volapük in May 1879 and a full book in 1880. By 1889 there were an estimated 283 clubs, and the language claimed nearly a million adherents.

**[f.f. expl.]** A global language that collapsed within a decade. Internal conflicts and schisms, together with the rise of Esperanto, drew speakers away.

## vstg-0158 — Latino sine flexione, Peano's simplified Latin

**Año:** 1903  ·  **Nivel:** documented  ·  **Estado:** Verificada con Wikipedia: publicada en 1903; versión simplificada del latín; no hay adopción formal.

**[desc.]** Giuseppe Peano published De Latino sine flexione in 1903, a simplified form of Latin that keeps its vocabulary. At the 1907 Paris delegation Peano declared for it, but he could not take part in the final vote.

**[f.f. expl.]** A Latin-based auxiliary language meant to be learned in weeks. Mathematicians were largely put off by its artificial appearance, and it was never adopted as an international language.

## vstg-0159 — Ro, an a priori language of categories

**Año:** 1906  ·  **Nivel:** documented  ·  **Estado:** Verificada con Wikipedia: Foster, 1904 inicio, 1906 primer folleto; resolución de 1914 enviada a comisión; sin adopción documentada.

**[desc.]** Rev. Edward Powell Foster began building Ro in 1904 and issued the first booklet about it in 1906. Words are formed from a category system, so related concepts share prefixes; for example, words beginning with 'bofo-' denote colours.

**[f.f. expl.]** A language whose vocabulary was meant to be logical from the ground up. The text finds no evidence that it was ever adopted; the only official act was a 1914 House resolution sent to committee.

## vstg-0165 — Occidental, later Interlingue

**Año:** 1922  ·  **Nivel:** documented  ·  **Estado:** Verificada con Wikipedia: anuncio en 1922 en Tallin; cambio de nombre en 1949; prohibición en Alemania, Austria y Checoslovaquia; declive tras 1951.

## vstg-0166 — Yañalif, the Latin alphabet for Soviet Turkic languages

**Año:** 1927  ·  **Nivel:** documented  ·  **Estado:** Verificada con Wikipedia: adopción en 1927; versión final en 1928; prohibición en 1939; uso hasta enero de 1940.

**[desc.]** Yañalif, a Latin-based alphabet devised for Turkic languages, was adopted in Tatarstan in 1927; its final version came in 1928. It was used for Tatar and Bashkir and other Soviet languages.

**[f.f. expl.]** Soviet Latinisation ended with a ban. The government prohibited Yañalif in 1939, and it remained in use until January 1940, when the switch to Cyrillic was complete. Learning two alphabets was considered problematic.

## vstg-0171 — The Sun Language Theory

**[desc.]** Turkish linguists of the Turkish Language Society developed the Sun Language Theory over about ten months in late 1935 and early 1936, presenting it at the Third Turkish Language Congress. It claims all languages descend from a proto-Turkic primal language.

## vstg-0178 — Lincos, a language for cosmic intercourse

**Año:** 1960  ·  **Nivel:** documented  ·  **Estado:** Verificada con Wikipedia: Freudenthal, 1960; Cosmic Call en 1999 desde Yevpatoria; repetido en 2003.
