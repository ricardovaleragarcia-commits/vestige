## vstg-0041 — Tsiolkovsky's Space Tower, 1895

- Código visible: vstg.1895
- Concepción: 1895 · Cierre: n/d · Distancia a 2020: 125
- [desc.] Konstantin Tsiolkovsky's idea of a tower rising from the Earth's surface to geostationary height, so that a payload could reach orbit without rockets.
- [f.f. expl.] A ladder to orbit: a tower rising from the ground to geostationary height, so that people and cargo could leave the Earth without rockets. It was never built; Tsiolkovsky's was a compression tower, and the tension-cable version studied today descends from Artsutanov (1960) and Pearson (1975).
- [Efecto en el presente — documented] The idea survives as the space elevator. Obayashi Corporation said in 2012 that it could build one by 2050 with carbon nanotubes; in 2014 Google X shelved its effort after finding that no carbon-nanotube strand longer than a metre had been made; in 2019 the International Academy of Astronautics published 'Road to the Space Elevator Era'.
- Fuente del efecto: Wikipedia, "Space elevator". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_elevator
- Fuente: Tsiolkovsky, K. (1895). Грёзы о Земле и небе [Dreams of Earth and Heaven]. Wikipedia, "Space elevator". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_elevator
- Nota: Efecto presente verificado en Wikipedia (Obayashi 2012, Google X 2014, IAA 2019). Nota: la torre de Tsiolkovsky era de compresión; el cable actual desciende de Artsutanov y Pearson.

## vstg-0042 — Project Habbakuk, 1942–1943

- Código visible: vstg.1943.b
- Concepción: 1942 · Cierre: 1943 · Distancia a 2020: 78
- [desc.] A British plan, promoted by Geoffrey Pyke, for an enormous aircraft carrier built from Pykrete, a mix of ice and wood pulp. A 60-foot scale model was tested; the project was abandoned in 1943.
- [f.f. expl.] A warship hull made of ice strengthened with wood pulp: cheap, hard to sink and independent of scarce steel. The final Habbakuk board meeting of December 1943 concluded that Habbakuk II was impractical.
- [Efecto en el presente — documented] The sunken metal parts of the 1944 prototype remain in Patricia Lake, Alberta; the ice took three hot summers to melt. The material, named pykrete after Geoffrey Pyke, has been retested on television (MythBusters, 2009; BBC Bang Goes the Theory, 2010), and both hulls leaked.

## vstg-0043 — Silbervogel, 1944

- Código visible: vstg.1944.a
- Concepción: 1941 · Cierre: n/d · Distancia a 2020: 79
- [desc.] Eugen Sänger's design for a boost-glide spaceplane, the Silbervogel, meant to bomb New York from orbit. Designed for the German Reich; never flown.
- [f.f. expl.] A rocket-powered bomber that would skip along the upper atmosphere to strike the Americas. Sänger's 900-page proposal of 3 December 1941 was filed away for its size; a reduced version, UM 3538, was submitted in September 1944 and never built.

Its lifting-body principle foreshadowed the X-20 and the Space Shuttle.

## vstg-0046 — Project Sherwood, 1951–1958

- Código visible: vstg.1958.c
- Concepción: 1951 · Cierre: 1958 · Distancia a 2020: 69

## vstg-0047 — Project Chariot, 1958–1962

- Código visible: vstg.1958.d
- Concepción: 1958 · Cierre: 1962 · Distancia a 2020: 62
- [desc.] A proposed Atomic Energy Commission plan to excavate a harbour at Cape Thompson, Alaska, with a series of nuclear explosions. Cancelled in 1962 after protests from Iñupiat communities and scientists.

## vstg-0052 — Project Cybersyn, 1971-1973

- Código visible: vstg.1973.b
- Concepción: 1971 · Cierre: 1973 · Distancia a 2020: 49
- [desc.] Chile's economic decision-support system under Salvador Allende, designed by Stafford Beer: a telex network (Cybernet), statistical software (Cyberstride), an economic simulator (CHECO) and an operations room (Opsroom).
- [f.f. expl.] A planned economy steered in near real time, with factories linked by telex to a control room in Santiago. - [Efecto en el presente — attributed] The Guardian described the network as 'a sort of socialist internet, decades ahead of its time'. Leigh Phillips and Michał Rozworski discussed it in their 2019 book, and Evgeny Morozov's 2014 essay examined its link to big data and algorithmic monitoring.

## vstg-0056 — Project Iceworm, 1959–1966

- Código visible: vstg.1966.c
- Concepción: 1959 · Cierre: 1966 · Distancia a 2020: 61
- [desc.] A secret US Army plan to hide up to 600 nuclear missiles in tunnels beneath the Greenland ice sheet, under cover of the Camp Century base. Cancelled in 1966.
- [f.f. expl.] A launch network for nuclear missiles hidden under the Greenland ice, built behind the cover story of Camp Century. The ice moved faster than expected; Iceworm was cancelled in 1966 and the camp closed.
- [Efecto en el presente — documented] When the camp was decommissioned in 1967 its infrastructure and waste were left, on the assumption that snow would entomb them.
